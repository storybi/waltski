Winding down: the storm leaves Utah, and a quieter pattern takes over

Evan Thayer's Monday update says the storm was exiting after delivering impressive water totals statewide, though only the highest elevations turned it into meaningful snow. A calmer stretch follows, with the next chance for a more active pattern around mid-November.

Source: OpenSnow Utah Daily Snow

Mixed

A weak system Wednesday–Thursday brings only a dusting up high, then dry and milder

Tuesday stays cool, dry and stable, 5–10 degrees below normal. A moisture-starved Pacific Northwest system crosses northern Utah Wednesday into Thursday with a rain/snow line near 7,000–7,500 feet, a few hundredths to a quarter inch of water and about half an inch to 4 inches above 7,500–8,000 feet, favoring the upper Cottonwoods, so Snowbasin and the Ogden Valley should see only light amounts on the upper slopes. High pressure then dries things out through the weekend with near- to above-normal temperatures early next week; Snowbasin still targets a Nov 27 opening and Powder Mountain Dec 11.

FDA approval · Prostate cancer

FDA approves Pfizer's Talzenna plus Xtandi for earlier-stage metastatic prostate cancer

The approval covers hormone-sensitive metastatic prostate cancer with HRR gene mutations and rests on the phase 3 TALAPRO-3 trial of 599 men. Adding Talzenna cut the risk of progression or death by 52% versus Xtandi alone; median progression-free survival has not yet been reached on the combination, versus 45.8 months with Xtandi.

FDA approval · Psoriatic arthritis

FDA approves Sun Pharma's Ilumya for adults with active psoriatic arthritis

The IL-23 blocker, approved for plaque psoriasis since 2018, now also covers psoriatic arthritis after the phase 3 INSPIRE-1 and INSPIRE-2 trials beat placebo on 24-week ACR20 response. Small trial · Type 2 diabetes

Low-calorie diet plus exercise puts 54% of young adults' type 2 diabetes into remission

The RESET for Remission trial randomized 96 adults aged 18–45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes in the UK and Canada. After 24 weeks of an 800–900 calorie-a-day diet with supervised exercise, 54% were in remission versus about 4% on usual care, with 8.4 kg of weight loss versus 1.2 kg; the study was small and short, so lasting benefit is unproven.

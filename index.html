HBR · Strategy
Mixed

Why AI Spending Isn't Yet Showing Up in Shareholder Returns, and How to Fix It

A team from Boston Consulting Group studied more than 600 US public companies and found that about three-quarters have moved AI beyond pilots, yet as a group they have not earned better shareholder returns than their industry peers. The authors argue that piling on more tools is not the answer. Winners pick one of three routes to value: doing existing work more efficiently, improving current products, or creating new offerings. Each requires different capabilities, from redesigned workflows and broad AI fluency across the workforce to specialist talent for building new businesses. It is a timely lens after a week when investors punished heavy AI spenders.

ESMO late-breaker · Melanoma

Personalized mRNA cancer vaccine cuts melanoma recurrence 42% in a phase 3 trial

In Merck and Moderna's 1,137-patient INTerpath-001 study, adding intismeran, a vaccine built from each patient's own tumor mutations, to Keytruda after surgery lowered the risk of recurrence or death by 42%; 82.2% were recurrence-free at two years vs. 70.0% on Keytruda alone. Survival data are not yet mature, side effects were mostly fever, injection-site pain and fatigue, and the companies are in talks with regulators.

Adding Amgen's tarlatamab extends survival by eight months in aggressive small cell lung cancer

In the 563-patient DeLLphi-305 trial, maintenance tarlatamab plus durvalumab after first-line chemo produced median survival of 21.1 months vs. 12.7 months on durvalumab alone, a 58% lower risk of death, and 79% of patients were alive at one year vs. 53%. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in about half of patients but was almost all low grade.

Observational study · Alzheimer's

A blood test for p-tau217 flags Alzheimer's-related decline up to a decade before symptoms

Pooling six studies of 2,684 cognitively healthy older adults, Mass General Brigham researchers found those with very high p-tau217 levels had about a 38% risk of impairment within 5 years and 78% within 10, independent of amyloid scans and APOE4 genes. Published in JAMA, the 10-year estimate rests on fewer data points, and the authors do not recommend routine screening until preventive treatments exist.

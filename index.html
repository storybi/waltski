Mixed

Managers Should Stop Fearing the Product Shutdown

A study of about 360 product and service shutdowns over nearly two decades found roughly two in five drew no measurable public reaction, and only about one in three of those that were noticed provoked a negative response. The author argues leaders should separate whether anyone will notice from whether they will care, scale the effort to that visibility, and help the small, loyal core of users transition so resources can shift to stronger products.

FDA approval · Rectal cancer

Immunotherapy alone may spare some rectal cancer patients surgery, chemo and radiation

The FDA granted accelerated approval to GSK's Jemperli (dostarlimab) as the only treatment for untreated stage II–III rectal cancer with mismatch-repair deficiency (about 3% of cases). In the 154-patient AZUR-1 trial, nearly three-quarters of patients still had not needed surgery, radiation or chemotherapy after more than two years. In an earlier 50-patient Memorial Sloan Kettering study, every tumor disappeared.

FDA approval · Hepatitis B

First antisense drug for hepatitis B could let some patients stop treatment

GSK's Hibsago (bepirovirsen) is approved alongside standard antivirals for adults without cirrhosis whose virus is already suppressed. In two placebo-controlled trials, 24% qualified to stop their hepatitis B medicine vs. none on placebo, and 19% kept the virus controlled 24 weeks after stopping all therapy. It carries a boxed warning for liver reactions and low platelets.

Pooled trials · Blood pressure

Skipping blood pressure pills erases much of their heart protection

An Oxford-led analysis of nine randomized trials with 91,339 people found that patients taking at least 80% of their doses had 11% fewer heart attacks, strokes and heart-failure events. Patients who took fewer showed no significant benefit. Adherence slipped from 88% in year one to 79% by year five.

Phase 3 topline · Skin disease

AbbVie's dual IL-1 blocker meets goal in painful skin disease hidradenitis suppurativa

In the 1,400-patient INTREPID trial, 31.4% of patients on lutikizumab reached the strict HiSCR75 response at week 16 vs. 23.4% on placebo. Patients on the drug also had less pain and fewer flares (18.4% vs. 28.1%). These are company-reported topline results with a modest margin; AbbVie plans regulatory filings next.

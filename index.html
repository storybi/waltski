US Growth Runs Hotter Than Expected Days Before the Midterms

The economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, up from 2.2% in the spring and ahead of the roughly 3% economists had penciled in. Consumer spending rose 3.8% despite war-driven energy costs, and business investment outside housing jumped 12.7%, led by a 37% surge in information-processing equipment for AI. Housing was the weak spot, shrinking 3.3% for its sixth decline in seven quarters as mortgage rates stay high. With inflation still running near 3.6%, the strong print gives the Fed little reason to ease and lands as a talking point five days before the Nov. 3 elections.

HBR · Leadership
Favorable

Your AI Roadmap May Already Exist in Your Most Curious Employees

Harvard Business School's Linda Hill and colleagues, drawing on research with nearly 10,000 leaders in 108 countries, find that organizations further along in digital transformation lean on "digital wayfinders": staff, often but not always junior, who experiment with new tools on their own. These people surface uses leaders miss and challenge stale assumptions, yet their habit of tinkering without permission and speaking bluntly can derail projects and irritate bosses. The authors recommend open two-way channels so their ideas get heard, trimming bureaucracy while setting clear business and ethical guardrails, and giving them visible sponsorship and a voice in real decisions.

Phase 3 · Type 2 diabetes

Lilly's triple-hormone shot beats Ozempic head to head on blood sugar and weight

In the 1,298-patient TRANSCEND-T2D-2 trial, people with type 2 diabetes on the top 12 mg dose of retatrutide cut A1C by 2.2 points vs. 1.8 on Ozempic and lost 20.9% of body weight vs. 9.4% over 80 weeks. Stomach side effects were common in all groups, and more people quit retatrutide for side effects (up to 7.9% vs. 2.7%). These are topline company results, not yet peer reviewed, and the drug is not approved.

FDA approval · Hepatitis B

FDA clears GSK's Hibsago, a new kind of hepatitis B drug aimed at a functional cure

Bepirovirsen is the first antisense drug approved for chronic hepatitis B; it blocks the virus's genetic instructions and lowers surface antigen levels. In two placebo-controlled trials, 24% of patients qualified to stop their usual antiviral pills (vs. none on placebo), and 19% still had the virus under control six months after stopping all treatment. It is for certain adults without cirrhosis and carries a boxed warning.

Merck's targeted chemo extends survival by about seven months in advanced endometrial cancer

In the 776-patient TroFuse-005 trial, previously treated patients given sacituzumab tirumotecan lived a median 19.0 months vs. 12.1 months on standard chemotherapy, a 32% lower risk of death, and went 7.2 vs. 3.9 months without progression. Observational · Dementia

Too little bright daylight is tied to higher dementia risk

A study of 87,577 adults who wore light-tracking wrist sensors for a median of eight years found that average daytime light above about 1,000 lux was linked to a 16% lower dementia risk, and under roughly 42 minutes a day of bright light was a stronger warning sign than several established risk factors. Published in General Psychiatry, it shows an association only; the authors say it does not prove more daylight prevents dementia.

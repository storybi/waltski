OpenSnow · Utah Daily Snow · Oct 30
Favorable

Even wetter: a very moist weekend storm, with significant snow possible on the high peaks

Evan Thayer now expects even more moisture from the weekend system, which brings plentiful rain and high-elevation snow to Utah. The storm starts warm and cools gradually, so snow levels drop as it goes: the high peaks could pick up significant snow, while lower elevations see smaller amounts. KSL's Friday model roundup has 4 to 8 inches for most higher terrain and 8 to 16 inches in the upper Cottonwoods.

Sources: OpenSnow Utah Daily Snow · KSL

NWS Salt Lake City's Snowbasin forecast has showers and possible thunderstorms today (high 51), then steady rain after midnight with half to three-quarters of an inch, and another 1 to 2 inches Sunday with thunder possible (high 43). The Economist · AI Policy
Mixed

The Economist Argues America Should Let China Copy Its AI

In a leader this week, The Economist pushes back on calls to outlaw "distillation," the practice of training cheaper models on the outputs of top American systems, which Chinese labs increasingly rely on. Its view is that annoyance at Anthropic and OpenAI is not grounds for a ban: distilled open models force price competition and spread low-cost AI to poorer countries. A companion science piece argues the habit of copying may end up holding China's own labs back rather than letting them leap ahead. For US tech investors the debate cuts both ways, since cheaper rival models squeeze pricing even as a ban would add new trade friction.

HBR · Workforce
Mixed

Microsoft's Mass Buyout Offer May Be a Template for Cost Cuts in the AI Era

HBR editor Daniel McGinn examines Microsoft's Oct. 26 offer of voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees, roughly a quarter of its global workforce, as a sign that profitable companies are trimming labor costs while pouring money into AI. Drawing on downsizing researcher Wayne Cascio, the piece notes that buyouts can protect morale because people choose when to go, but they risk losing top performers who have the best outside options. Workers weighing an offer should test it against their finances, career goals and readiness for change, and should not assume later packages will be as generous. For shareholders, leaner payrolls help margins, provided the company keeps the talent it needs.
